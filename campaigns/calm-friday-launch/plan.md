# The Calm Friday: Waitlist Launch Plan

Planning document only. No email in this plan goes to real people from this studio. Sending happens later, in a sending tool, once you approve the final drafts.

Related: `campaigns/calm-friday/brief.md` (the offer brief), `brand/voice.md` (voice rules).

## 1. Campaign overview

**Campaign name:** The Calm Friday: Your Spot Is Ready

**Summary:** A four-week, email-only launch that invites our 2,000 waitlist members to start The Calm Friday subscription, with a calm, no-pressure tone that suits our brand.

**Primary goal:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription between Monday 7 December 2026 and Sunday 3 January 2027.

**Secondary goals:**
- Teach every waitlist member how The Calm Friday works (credits, swaps, Calm Friday discount).
- Get at least 30 Past Sellers to list or swap an item during the campaign.
- Learn which segment responds best, to guide the next campaign.

### Dates

| | Date |
|---|---|
| Campaign starts (first Monday of next month) | Mon 7 Dec 2026 |
| Campaign ends | Sun 3 Jan 2027 |
| Holiday days with no sends | Thu 24 Dec to Sun 27 Dec, Fri 1 Jan |

### A note on the target

300 out of 2,000 is a 15% conversion rate. That is high for email, but possible for a warm waitlist, because these people already asked to hear from us. It depends on two things: the offer being clear and worth it, and the sign-up page being easy. If week 1 comes in well under target, see Risks (section 9).

### What we still need to confirm before writing emails

The offer brief (`campaigns/calm-friday/brief.md`) describes credits and a 15% Calm Friday discount, but it does not mention a subscription. Before any email is written we need:

1. **What the subscription is.** What does a subscriber get that a normal user does not?
2. **Price** and billing (monthly or yearly), in rupees.
3. **How the brief's offer fits in.** Are the 500 swap credits and the 15% off subscriber perks? Can they be used together?
4. **A waitlist thank-you.** Do waitlist members get anything extra (for example, first month at a lower price)? If yes, until when?
5. **Segment sizes.** How many people are in Past Buyers, Past Sellers and Quiet Users?
6. **Sign-up page.** Where does the "Start my subscription" button go?

Everything below assumes the answers will fit the brief. Items marked **[confirm]** change once we know.

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the waitlist. All of them gave us their email on purpose, so they are warm. They split into three groups.

### Past Buyers (primary)

People who have bought a pre-loved gadget from EasyBuy before.

- **Want:** good gadgets that are kind to their wallet; no surprises about condition.
- **Worry:** "Is a subscription worth it if I only buy now and then?"
- **Buying stage:** decision. They trust us already. They need to see the value in rupees.

### Past Sellers (primary)

People who have sold or swapped an item with us before.

- **Want:** a fair, easy way to turn old gadgets into something useful.
- **Worry:** "Will my item be accepted? Are credits really worth it?"
- **Buying stage:** decision. The swap credits are the strongest hook for them.

### Quiet Users (secondary)

People who joined the waitlist but have not bought or sold with us.

- **Want:** to understand what EasyBuy is and whether pre-loved is right for them.
- **Worry:** quality, and whether they will actually use a subscription.
- **Buying stage:** consideration. They need trust before they need an offer.

**Where they spend time:** their email inbox (our only channel for this campaign) and the EasyBuy website, where the sign-up page lives.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** The Calm Friday is a calmer way to buy and swap pre-loved gadgets, and as a waitlist member, your spot is ready.

| # | Supporting message | Proof point |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Good for your wallet.** Members save on every pre-loved gadget. | 15% off on Calm Friday; 100 credits = Rs 1,000. **[confirm]** |
| 2 | **Your old gadgets are worth something.** Swap or sell an item and earn credits. | 500 credits (Rs 5,000) per accepted item. **[confirm if flat]** |
| 3 | **Quality you can trust.** We only accept items of decent to good quality, and we describe each one honestly. | Our item check process. Show what we check; do not promise perfection. |
| 4 | **No rush, ever.** The Calm Friday is the opposite of shopping-day chaos. Take your time. | The tone of every email: no countdowns, no pressure. |

### Message by segment

- **Past Buyers:** "You already know pre-loved works. The Calm Friday makes your next buy kinder on your wallet." Lead with message 1.
- **Past Sellers:** "Your next swap earns 500 credits." Lead with message 2.
- **Quiet Users:** "No pressure. Here is what EasyBuy is and how The Calm Friday works." Lead with messages 3 and 4.

### Voice checklist for every email

- Open with **Dear [name],** and close with **Regards, The EasyBuy Team**.
- Say pre-loved (or twice-loved, pre-adored, secondhandsome). Never the words on the avoid list in `brand/voice.md`.
- No urgency words, even in the final week. We remind, we do not push.
- None of the claims in `brand/voice.md` section 4. Describe condition honestly instead.
- Short sentences, simple words, one clear button per email.

## 4. Channel strategy

### Email (the only channel)

- **Why it fits:** every waitlist member gave us their email and expects to hear from us. It costs almost nothing, and we can tailor each email to the segment.
- **Format:** short, mostly-text emails with one button. Plain layout, logo at the top, no heavy images (they load slowly and look less personal).
- **Effort:** medium. About 7 emails, with segment versions of 3 of them.
- **Budget:** all of it. See section 8.

### Supporting pieces (not channels, but needed)

- **Sign-up page** on the website. Every email links here. Must be live before the first send.
- **Welcome email** sent automatically when someone subscribes.

We are not using social, ads or partners in this campaign. If the target looks out of reach after week 2, a short post on our own social pages is the cheapest extra step (see Risks).

### Sending rules

- Anyone who subscribes stops getting campaign emails and gets the welcome email instead.
- Anyone who unsubscribes is removed at once.
- **Quiet Users get fewer emails:** E1, E2, E3, E4 and E6 only. Fewer emails to less active people protects our sender reputation.
- Send in the morning, local time.
- Most people get 5 to 7 emails over 4 weeks, about 1 to 2 a week.

### Dependencies

- The 6 open questions must be answered before drafting.
- Sign-up page must be live before E1.
- Tracking links must work (tested on your own inbox) before E1.
- The wording of E7 depends on whether there is a waitlist-only offer.

## 9. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | What could happen | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| **The offer is not clear** | The brief does not describe the subscription yet. Vague emails convert poorly. | Answer the 6 open questions before drafting. No drafting starts until they are answered. |
| **The target is ambitious** | 15% conversion is high for email. | Check at end of week 1. If under 90 subscriptions: rewrite E3 around the strongest segment, add a second resend to non-openers, and consider one post on our own social pages. |
| **Holiday timing** | Weeks 3 and 4 fall over Christmas and New Year, when inboxes are busy and people are away. | Lighter week 3, no sends 24 to 27 Dec or 1 Jan, and a fresh-start angle for week 4. Plan to get most sign-ups in weeks 1 and 2. |
| **Quiet Users hurt our sender reputation** | Old or unused addresses bounce or mark us as spam, so all our emails start landing in spam. | Remove bounced addresses before launch, send Quiet Users fewer emails, and stop emailing anyone who has not opened by E4. |
| **Pressure creeps into the copy** | In the final week it is tempting to use urgency words, which goes against our voice. | Run `/marketing:brand-review` on every email, especially E7. |
| **Quality claims go too far** | Saying items are perfect could break trust and our claims rules. | Talk about our item check and honest descriptions, never about perfect condition. |
